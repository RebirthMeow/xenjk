## What this says

1. **Stock sabers depend heavily on tickrate.** This confirms the report. At 125 Hz stock, a medium click deals
   about 1.6x the damage of 30 Hz into a dummy, about 2.2x in a clash, and the clash always ends with both fighters
   hit, never bouncing. The ±0.0 intervals show these outcomes no longer depend on swing phase at 125 Hz.
2. **The 33 ms saber pass alone does not reproduce 30 Hz.**
   - With it on, 125 and 250 Hz land about halfway between stock 30 and stock 125, and they agree with each other.
   - The first hit lands about 40 ms earlier than at 30 Hz.
   - So something outside the gated pass still runs per frame and changes where the blade is, or when the swing
     starts.
   - The design note's candidate is the skeleton: `G_UpdateClientAnims` still runs every frame, and animations start
     at the frame that notices them.
   - At 40 Hz (25 ms frames, with uneven 25/50 ms pass intervals), dummy damage happens to match 30 Hz, but clash
     outcomes are the furthest of any rate. Pass jitter matters too.
3. **`block_medium` equals `dummy_medium`.** Under SP-style damage an idle saber blocks only by contact, and at this
   geometry it is never in the way.

## Saber step, round 1 (same day)

Each change was judged on dummy_medium and clash_medium. Evidence is in `.codex-research/saberlab-*` on the main
checkout's D: drive.

**The traces found three reasons the 33 ms pass alone missed 30 Hz.**
1. **The skeleton updated every frame.** Animations started on the frame that noticed them. Fix:
   `G_UpdateClientAnims`, and the torso/legs swing smoothing in `G_G2PlayerAngles`, now run on pass frames only.
   The smoothing steps a fixed `FRAMETIME` per call.
2. **The Ghoul2 clock is the engine's previous-frame time.** Bolt and collision queries ignore the time the game
   passes in. At 30 Hz a pass sees skeletons 33 ms back; at 125 Hz, 8 ms back.
   - Fix: a new engine export, `G2API_SetTime`, at the end of `gameImport_t`, announced by the read-only cvar
     `sv_g2ClockImport 1`. The game calls it only when that cvar is set, so this game module still runs on stock
     engines.
   - During a pass on a faster tick, skeletons are posed at the pass's nominal 33 ms-grid time − 33, and new
     animations start at the nominal time.
   - At 30 Hz this does nothing, and the self-check still passes (`saberlab-selfcheck-05`).
3. **Rejected:** a start-time shift without the engine clock (poses barely moved), a per-client animation history,
   and pass rules other than nearest frame. They are measured in the folders `saberlab-anim-*`, `g2clock-02/03` and
   `net-03`.

**Result with realistic delivery** (`saberlab-net-01`; lat=30, latjit=20, 300 reps; damage relative to 30 Hz):

| Rate | Dummy damage | Clash damage | Clash outcomes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 90 Hz (11 ms frames, 33 = 3 frames) | +3% | −1% | match |
| 333 Hz (3 ms, 33 = 11 frames) | 0% | 0% | match |
| 250 Hz | −5% | −12% | match |
| 125 Hz | −17% | −14% | match |
| 60 Hz | −20% | −18% | reflections differ |

With zero-latency packets, outcomes match exactly at 90, 125, 250 and 333 Hz (`saberlab-g2clock-01`).

**What remains** is the pass reading player state (`saberMove`, attack or return) as of its actual frame. That frame
can be up to F/2 from the nominal mark, so the attack window shifts a few ms and the damaging passes change. Damage
falls the later the pass runs: about −10% on the last frame at or before the mark, −16% on the nearest frame, and
−28% on the first frame at or after it.

**To close it:** run the pass against the player state as of the mark. Or choose a tickrate whose frame divides
33 ms (90 or 333 Hz), where everything measured already matches within 3%.

## Next: 125 Hz within 5% (owner 2026-10-22: "do 125"; 5% is the acceptable saber margin)

The plan was set, and no code was written. Hold commands so that each pass sees exactly the commands a 30 Hz pass
at its mark would see:

1. Pass on the first frame at or after each 33 ms mark (`G_TickSaberPass`: `level.time >= level.saberPassNext`).
2. Hold late commands.
   - Between frames, when the next frame will carry a pass (`level.time + frame >= saberPassNext`), a command with
     `serverTime > saberPassNext` is queued per client instead of run. This applies in `ClientThink`, and in the lab's
     `LabDeliver`, which calls `ClientThink_real` directly.
   - The queue is flushed (`pers.cmd` = cmd, `ClientThink_real`) after that frame's entity loop and before
     `ClientEndFrame`.
   - Commands are delayed at most one frame (8 ms at 125 Hz). Pmove physics is unaffected because it uses the
     command's own `serverTime`.
3. Frame order to respect (`g_main.c` ~3378): per client, `WP_ForcePowersUpdate`, `WP_SaberPositionUpdate` (the
   pass), `WP_SaberStartMissileBlockCheck`, then `G_RunClient`. `ClientEndFrame` comes after the loop (~3442).
4. Judge on dummy_medium and clash_medium at 30/90/125/250 with `--set lat=30 --set latjit=20 --reps 300`. Target:
   125 within 5% of 30 Hz on damage, outcomes within interval. The self-check must still pass.

The current code state is the best measured configuration: nearest-frame passes, skeleton and smoothing gated to
passes, engine Ghoul2 clock import. The rejected experiments are reverted.
