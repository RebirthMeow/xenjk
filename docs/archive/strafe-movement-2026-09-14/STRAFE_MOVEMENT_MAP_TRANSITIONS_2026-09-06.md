# Automatic real-map transition proposals — 2026-09-06

This offline prototype converts the hash-bound Detour support inventory into possible one-gap transition descriptors. It supplies candidates for a later native feedback fixture. It does not establish that a gap is open, reachable, safe, or useful.

The generator considers exposed edges from distinct, nonadjacent ground polygons. It uses a 384-unit spatial grid; for each source edge, its deterministic candidate universe is the nearest 32 exposed-edge midpoints returned by that index. Same-polygon and adjacent-polygon edges consume this 32-pair examination cap. It accepts closest edge points with horizontal separation from 32 through 384 units and height difference at most 64 units. Each edge must be at least 24 units long. The nearest 16 pairs that pass only the gap and height tests are retained before the later approach and onward filters; pairs rejected by those later filters are not backfilled. The source polygon centroid must provide at least 16 units of progress toward the source edge. The destination must have an ordinary adjacent polygon centroid within 192 units and ahead of the transition direction. Every rejection inside the declared 32-edge candidate universe, including the 16-pair overflow, is recorded by stable directed edge-pair ID and reason. Per-source and aggregate counts state how many indexed edges were examined and how many were unexamined because of the 32-pair cap. More distant unexamined edges are outside this bounded experiment rather than silently classified as failures.

Each proposal records source and destination polygon/edge identities, exact edge endpoints and closest points, polygon-centroid approach and landing points, a launch plane, and an onward point/plane chosen from Detour adjacency. The construction operates in world coordinates and preserves rotated polygons; it does not replace them with axis-aligned support boxes. All ordering and tie breaking are deterministic.

The bounds are deliberately broad candidate-generation limits rather than movement claims. A Detour boundary can represent a wall, ledge, void, collision patch, tile artifact, hazard, or mover interface. Polygon centroids and edges come from rasterized navigation support and do not prove native hull clearance. The next fixture must validate WORLD support association, stationary clearance, hazards, approach and onward corridors, open flight volume, landing contact, full feedback movement, and forecast/live parity. Failed candidates remain evidence.

This is preparation for a one-transition research fixture. Support point checks establish local facts; only native Pmove can establish transition validity. No generated descriptor is a route, no two transitions are composed, and no production Bot2 behavior consumes this output.

Each artifact binds the input support hash, source BSP/navmesh identities, generator hash, and this protocol hash. It snapshots the exact generator and protocol bytes under its own `declarations` directory.
