# From OpenJK to xenjk

xenjk is an OpenJK fork for Jedi Academy multiplayer. It runs JKSunny's Vulkan renderer (`rd-vulkan`), rebuilt
around characters and levels taken from modern games. This guide is for a developer who knows stock OpenJK, or a
derivative such as EternalJK, japro, TaystJK or JKSunny's fork, and wants either to follow xenjk as a whole or
to take one piece of it into their own tree.

Every chapter stands on its own and follows the same layout:

- **What it does**: the mechanism.
- **Take this piece**: the files to copy, the stock functions they hook, the cvars, the data formats and the
  build steps.
- **Do it yourself**: for the pipelines, the steps from a game you own to something that loads in xenjk.
- **Limits and open issues**.
- **Further reading**: the dated records underneath.

The records in `docs/` keep the full history, the measurements and the dead ends. The guide is the path through them.

The pipelines (character conversion, both level ports) are Python and Blender tools in `tools/`. They write files
that stock JKA formats or the renderer read, so you can run them against xenjk's engine or lift them alone.
The render-side pieces need `rd-vulkan`. A tree that only has rd-vanilla or rend2 can still take the gameplay BSPs,
the converted models without their PBR maps, and the engine and gameplay pieces listed below.

## Assets stay yours

The pipelines read game files from your own installed copy and write the results to your machine. Nothing converted,
neither a level nor a character, goes into this repository or gets published, and nothing in this guide needs one.
When a chapter says "your extraction folder", it means the folder where you unpacked your own copy.

## How xenjk keeps a piece liftable

These conventions run through the code. They are how you find the edges of a piece:

- **New files over edits.** A feature lives in its own files where it can: `g_tick.c`, `g_labs.c`, `cg_signals.c`,
  `cg_modelwarm.c`, `cg_xenanims.c`, `cm_chunk.cpp`, `sv_chunk.cpp`, `vk_scene*.cpp`, `vk_dds.cpp`,
  `tr_charshadow.cpp`, `bot2_*.c`. Stock files get short calls into them. A 2026-11-19 review moved several
  features out of stock files into their own to make them easier to take; each chapter's file tables name the
  result, and the review's Liftability sections (`docs/review/2026-11-19/`) say what is still tangled.
- **Hooks are marked.** A change to a stock file carries `xenjk` in a nearby comment (`// xenjk: ...`,
  `// tick: <area> (g_tick.c)` for tick independence). `rg -n xenjk <file>` lists a file's hooks, and
  `git log --oneline -- <file>` dates them.
- **One switch per feature.** Each feature has a cvar, and 0 brings back the stock behaviour wherever that is
  possible. [xenjk-cvars.md](../xenjk-cvars.md) lists every cvar xenjk added since the Vulkan import, with its
  default, flags and description. It is generated by `tools/cvar_list.py`, so regenerate it rather than editing it.
- **API additions are appended and advertised.** The module APIs (`cgameImport_t`, `uiImport_t`, the renderer's
  `refexport_t`) gain functions at the end of their `ext` structs. The engine announces them through a cvar, and the
  module checks for it before calling. For example, the cgame calls the player warm-up functions only when the engine
  sets `cl_cgameWarmApi` (`codemp/cgame/cg_public.h`). So a cgame built from xenjk still runs on an engine
  without the addition, and the reverse.
- **rd-vulkan was imported in one commit**: JKSunny's renderer at e4fe7581. Everything in `codemp/rd-vulkan`
  after that commit is xenjk's: `git diff e4fe7581 --stat -- codemp/rd-vulkan` sizes it, and
  `git log e4fe7581.. -- <file>` explains one file.
- **AI policy and shared kernels are split.** Under `codemp/game/`, `ai_bot2*` holds the bot AI's policy and its
  integration with the game, and `bot2_*` holds the reusable kernels; see the
  [Bot2 map](../../codemp/game/ai_bot2_README.md).
- **Generated files are generated.** Don't edit them by hand; edit their sources and rerun the generator:
  - `codemp/rd-vulkan/shaders/spirv/shader_data.c` (SPIR-V, `tools/renderer_spirv.py`);
  - the scene and cloth shaders (`tools/scene_spirv.py`, `tools/cloth_spirv.py`);
  - `docs/xenjk-cvars.md` (`tools/cvar_list.py`).

## Building

The build is OpenJK's CMake build with three more targets:

- **`xenjk.x86` / `xenjk.x86_64`**: the client under its own name, built beside `openjk.*`. An executable named
  `xenjk.*` is a "branded" binary (`Sys_IsBrandedBinary`, `shared/sys/sys_main.cpp`). It keeps its home in
  `fs_basepath`, so the 32- and 64-bit clients share one `base/openjk.cfg` and nothing goes to the user's
  profile folder.
- **`rd-vulkan_x86` / `rd-vulkan_x86_64`**: the renderer (`BuildMPRdVulkan`).
- **xenui** (`BuildXenUI`): RmlUi and FreeType inside the MP UI module.

One option trims the game module: `BuildGameLabs` (default ON) compiles the console-only measurement labs (the saber
lab and bench, the hit-coverage probe and the level-port trace checks, `codemp/game/g_labs.h`). Stock files call five
hooks in `g_labs.c`; with it OFF they do nothing and the lab commands are unknown. The level-port chapters' engine
traces (`levelcheck.py ... --engine`) need it ON.

xenjk builds every multiplayer target for Win32 and x64:

```
cmake -S . -B build_x64 -G "Visual Studio 17 2022" -A x64 -DBuildSPEngine=OFF -DBuildSPGame=OFF -DBuildSPRdVanilla=OFF
cmake --build build_x64 --config Release
```

Use `-A Win32` for the 32-bit build. The 64-bit client is the default because the converted levels and characters
press on the 32-bit address space: the Watson slice of Night City held 1.9 GB in the 32-bit client. The 64-bit modules are `*x86_64.dll`. They sit
in `base/` beside the 32-bit ones, and the 64-bit client, renderer and SDL2 sit in their own `bin64/` folder. How
the native test builds are bound and checked is in the
[verification reference](../archive/strafe-movement-2026-09-14/STRAFE_MOVEMENT_VERIFICATION.md).
