# New maps: Taloraan, Carratos, Metalorn v2; slow drops braked late

Recorded 2026-09-25. Owner question: how robust is the movement on maps it
has never seen? Maps `mp/ctf_taloraan`, `mp/ctf_carratos` and
`ctf_metalorn_v2` (owner: the latest Metalorn), each with a navmesh.

## Fixtures

`tools/movement_cases_from_bsp.py`, Kejim's declared set (36 cases a map),
heights from the engine's seed cells (`--graph-world`, a world that loaded the
map): `tools/fixtures/movement/{taloraan,carratos,metalorn_v2}/cases.json`.
The offline brush trace agrees on Taloraan and Carratos; on Metalorn both
`respawn_5` poses sit 32 units higher by the engine (a floor the brush trace
does not see), so the engine's heights are used. Graphs at load: Taloraan
8,891 cells, 259 edges (182 climbs, 61 drops); Carratos 11,670 cells, 119
edges; Metalorn 15,829 cells, 568 edges (324 climbs, 189 drops); no seed
refused on any.

Carratos's level-1 stand trips ran 21.4-27.0 s, a 5.6 s spread between passes

others' (267-271 cells against 192-205); the spread came from slow drops.

## The defect: slow drops braked from the start

Slow drops fire far more on these maps than on Dantooine: 13-50 commits a
floor against 7-8. Every one asked for 150 u/s from the first run-up frame:
half of 199 slow taps ran over 150 units that way, and a Carratos stand trip
spent 2.15 s on one 342-unit run-up to a drop at (-62, 336, 160).

`bot_moveDropBrake` (units; **96 by default, owner 2026-09-25**, 0 off): the
slow pass first offers its 15 candidates run in at the usual speed and braked
to the approach speed only this far before the takeoff (`approachAt`, a plan
field; a step off's ledge is the edge's takeoff), then the 15 slowed from the
start. The forecast judges both groups; the soonest to finish wins.

596 passing worlds, **zero deaths, zero failures**; the 76 incompletes are all
start-class misses (Metalorn misses its pin most). Dantooine never chose a
late brake (its narrow floor needs the early one, still offered), and Kejim's
drop searches never fail, so both are unchanged: the gates re-taken on
`dropslow-build-20260924-b` ([record](2026-09-25-gates-slowdrop.md)) are
unaffected. Taloraan's slower pairs are route divergence, not a worse drop:
at the first drop off the blue stand the late-braked tap finishes its own
candidate sooner, lands elsewhere, and the trip then takes two more slow
drops, 2,025 ms slower (`blue_out_L2_stand` class 150); the same mechanism
gains 1,025 on `red_out_L2_stand`.

Build `dropbrake-build-20260925-b` with the cvar unset reproduces the 96 arm:
Metalorn levels 2-3 at class 50, 19 of 19 shared cases identical
(`.codex-research/dropbrake-default-20260925-a/`).

## Other findings

- **Candidates ranked by their own landing.** A drop that finishes its
  candidate sooner can enter a slower route (Taloraan above). Not new; these
  maps make it visible.
- **Full-speed drops fail often here.** The slow pass runs only after all 12
  full-speed candidates fail, and it ran up to 50 times a floor: the drop
  edges' takeoffs or offsets fit these maps poorly, and each failure costs a
  full search first. Diagnosed and measured in the
  [drop-aim record](2026-09-25-drop-aim.md).
- **After completion.** On ctf4 `red_out_L3_respawn4` (slow drops on) the bot,
  its objective done and with no path, ran on at 780 u/s into the base pit.
  Outside every benchmark row; a real game continues past that point.
  Diagnosed and repaired in the [arrive-safe record](2026-09-25-arrive-safe.md).
- **Runner.** Renaming a missed world's directory failed twice on a Windows
  lock (WinError 5) and killed the arm; `movement_bench.py` now retries the
  rename for 20 s. Both arms were re-run (`*.lockfail` retained).
