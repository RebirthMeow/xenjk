# Paired policy replay and the no-Force time gap, 2026-09-17

> **Reviewed and corrected, 2026-09-17.** This record retains the original
> campaigns and analyzer-v1 tables. The active timing and inference record is
> [the replay correction](2026-09-17-replay-correction.md). Launch confirmation
> was mistaken for the earlier jump-command boundary; sections 2 and 6 are
> superseded timing analysis. Blue and small-seed red contain B-only rescues,
> and censored B results cannot count as rejections. No unique root cause or
> completed replay milestone is established. Follow [HANDOFF.md](../HANDOFF.md)
> for successor trials and prospective capture of discarded merge records.

Executes [the 2026-09-16 handoff](../HANDOFF.md). The question is why the
state planner's roughly 13-second estimate becomes a roughly 19-second bot
run on a stationary no-Force start at the home stand, ending at the enemy
flag pickup. Three conclusions are kept apart throughout: **replay
experiment completed**, **implementation qualified**, and **sub-15-second
target achieved**.

## 1. What was built

`bot_moveReplay` (`codemp/game/bot2_move.c`), off by default, so ordinary
play, candidate selection and timing are unchanged:

- **bit 1** prints `[REPLAY]` records: `kind=path` and one `kind=path_edge`
  per planned edge (a complete edge-record snapshot bound to a new
  `path.generation`, because learning, merging and measured-cost updates
  change an edge under a fixed index); `kind=root`, selected live player
  fields, executor diagnostics, selected Force fields, view and controller yaw,
  command time, jump latch, animation, plan, permit and spend policy at a
  candidate boundary; `kind=cand` per judged candidate with its construction,
  rollout measurements, margin and whether it was committed; `kind=stage`
  per executed crossing, against what the committed rollout predicted and
  what the graph charged.
- **bit 2** adds the observe-only **measured-policy arm**. Both arms are
  built from one root, with the same hop counts, run-ups, first-frame
  semantics, inputs, spend policy, time step, walk reference, admission,
  post-landing hold and search order. Only the heading differs: arm A (the
  control, the only arm that can commit) aims from the live root at the
  edge's stored landing; arm B rides the edge's stored control heading
  (`graphEdge_t.dir`). Arm B is excluded from the verdict ranking, from the
  `stateAdmitted` tally that decides stale/refused, and from the walk
  horizon, so a longer arm-B rollout cannot change a control judgement.

`tools/movement_replay.py` (analyzer version 1 at original collection) reads a retained case
directory and writes `segments.csv`, `transitions.csv`, `paired_arms.csv`,
`runup_pairs.csv`, `chain_breaks.csv`, `replay.json` and a readable
`report.txt`. It starts no world and edits none.

**Observe-only, demonstrated.** With both bits set the instrumented build
reproduces the retained times to the millisecond: red outbound **19,150 ms**
and blue outbound **19,925 ms** on the large seed (identical to
`move-review-20260916-seeded-b`), and **19,775 ms** on the small clean seed
(identical to `move-review-20260916-explore-replay-a`).

A first instrumented build crashed the game DLL at
`.codex-research/move-replay-20260917T035808532Z-red-probe` and is retained
as a failed run: `playerState_t.speed` is a float and was printed with
`%d`, which shifted every later vararg and passed an integer to `%s`. It is
not evidence of anything about movement.

## 4. The measured first failure

The retained rows show entry-speed and landing-position differences for
planned hops. They motivate a composition investigation, but do not isolate
one cause. Position, heading, policy construction, merged records and state
keys can also affect the exit. Several first hops miss the reach too, and
not every second-hop entry lies outside the speed band.

Red outbound (`entry` is speed at the live launch-confirmation event,
not a full captured pre-command entry; `rec_entry` is the edge's recorded entry, `land_err` the distance from the live landing to the edge's
recorded landing; `GRAPH_STATE_REACH` is 48 units):

| level | hop | edge | entry | rec_entry | land_err | next edge reachable |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 4700 | 0 | 50732 | 250.1 | 241.9 | 47.4 u | yes |
| 5750 | 1 | 26663 | 364.7 | 433.1 | 70.2 u | **no** |
| 7100 | 0 | 36420 | 255.2 | 250.7 | 37.8 u | yes |
| 7925 | 1 | 38065 | 388.3 | 419.9 | 117.8 u | **no** |
| 11925 | 0 | 55745 | 255.1 | 250.5 | 8.2 u | yes |
| 12850 | 1 | 34074 | 253.5 | 413.2 | 106.0 u | **no** |
| 13675 | 1 | 51385 | 365.2 | 507.2 | 262.5 u | **no** |
| 15050 | 0 | 59794 | 250.2 | 250.1 | 55.4 u | **no** |

Blue outbound is the same shape: 11 executed planned edges, median landing
error 44.7 u, maximum 150.4 u, five outside the reach.

The source reason is exact. `PlannedStrafe` chooses the chained second edge
with

```c
if(n && n->tap && !n->disabled)*k2Out=k+1;
```

and nothing else. The first hop is at least held to the takeoff window; the
second hop has **no envelope test at all**. The state search, by contrast,
chained the two edges only because `Graph_StrafeAdmits` admitted the second
from the first's *recorded* exit (`StateStrafeEdges` sets
`next.speed=edge->exitSpeed`). Live, the first hop exits up to 160 u/s below
that recorded entry, the second lands 70 to 262 units from its record, and
the edge after it falls outside the 48-unit reach its own admission needs.

The following observations need separate attribution; they do not by
themselves prove which preceding transition caused each break:

- The plan goes **stale** (`envelope=0`) and the path is searched again.
- `StateLink` sets `s.tap=0` after any walk link, and `Graph_StrafeAdmits`
  refuses a `tap` edge from a non-landing state, so once the bot has walked
  a single frame the chain can never be re-entered as the tap it was
  learned as.
- The original event-confirmation calculation below is superseded by
  analyzer v2 for ground duration. Across red outbound there are 19
  landings, 11 chained on the very next frame and **8 broken**; the broken
  ones hold the bot on the ground for 2,575 ms and shed 1,435.7 u/s in
  total, each relaunching at about 250 u/s, the ground run cap. Blue
  outbound: 19 landings, 10 chained, 9 broken, 3,175 ms of ground and
  1,543.4 u/s shed.

## 5. Hypotheses and limits of the retained tests

- **Mispriced flights or lost approach/braking time.** Not ruled out by
  the original partition: its launch boundary was late by one frame. Use
  corrected intervals and distinguish individual flights from route cost.
- **A replay that does not reproduce the learned policy.** Still open:
  blue and small-seed red have B-only admissions, and censored B outcomes
  need independent reference forecasts (section 3).
- **Spurious forecast trigger exclusions.** Largely rejected. With
  `bot_forecastTriggerLog` on, red outbound's refusals are 214 flag-stand
  triggers, 147 `trigger_hurt` and 18 `item_medpak_instant`. Entity 48 is a
  map-wide `trigger_hurt` spanning `absmin=(-770,-1594,6)` to
  `absmax=(3394,5754,706)`; the refusals against it are at z of about 720,
  a rollout that has already fallen roughly 1,100 units off the route.
  Those refusals are **correct**, and no exclusion was turned off. The 18
  item refusals are a real but small cost and are left alone; `bot_forecastItems`
  exists for them and was not enabled, because turning an exclusion off as a
  speed fix is out of scope here.
- **The corridor rule throws away momentum.** Not established. In 13 of 64
  sibling pairs the tap-at-once candidate is refused while its 24-unit
  run-up sibling is admitted, 8 of those as `off_path`, and the run-up sheds
  a median 110.7 u/s of launch speed. But measured against the objective in
  force at the time, those refused fast hops close **+356 u** more than the
  admitted run-ups on red outbound and **-2,318 u less** on blue outbound,
  where the worst of them lands off the graph entirely at 20.5 u/s. The
  corridor rule is rejecting a mixture of genuinely bad hops and genuinely
  better ones; one-hop progress is not route time, and this is recorded as
  an open question, not a defect.

## 6. Original gap decomposition (superseded)

The following table and arithmetic retain analyzer-v1 attribution only.
The correction record supersedes the flight/ground rows and derived terms;
matching their sum to elapsed time does not prove a root cause.

The planner's whole-path estimate, the sum of the one-hop flight costs
inside it, and the actual route elapsed time are three different quantities.
Taken from the `kind=path` and `kind=path_edge` snapshots of the path the
bot actually started on:

| | red outbound | blue outbound |
|---|---:|---:|
| whole-path estimate | 13,337 ms | 13,001 ms |
| sum of its edge flight costs | 12,925 ms (16 edges) | 12,300 ms (15 edges) |
| everything else it prices (walk links) | 412 ms | 701 ms |
| of those edges, landing-frame taps | 14 of 16 | 14 of 15 |
| actual elapsed | 19,150 ms | 19,925 ms |
| actual flight | 15,725 ms | 16,025 ms |
| actual ground | 3,425 ms | 3,900 ms |
| hop landings executed | 21 | 26 |

The original analyzer-v1 arithmetic assigned red's 5,813 ms gap as follows
(superseded; do not use as current component measurements):

- **+3,013 ms of unplanned ground.** The estimate prices 412 ms of walk
  links because 14 of its 16 edges are landing-frame taps: it assumes an
  unbroken chain that pays no ground time and no friction. Live, 8 of 19
  landings break the chain and the bot stands for 3,425 ms.
- **+2,800 ms of extra flight.** 21 hops were flown where 16 were priced.
  The extra hops are the re-accelerations and greedy straight hops that
  follow each break; their individual flight times are correct (section 2).

The two original terms sum to the elapsed gap by construction. Their
labels depend on corrected launch timing, and the sum cannot identify why
one route differs from the graph estimate. The two-hop plan limit and the
entry/exit mismatches are candidate explanations, not a demonstrated single
root cause. Further controlled successor and merge-policy experiments are
required.

## 7. The changes under test

### 7.1 `bot_moveChainAdmit`, off by default

Applies the search's own admission
test to the state the candidate's own native rollout measured at the first
landing:

```c
exit.speed=r->measure.landSpeed[0];
exit.dir=r->measure.landDir[0]; exit.tap=1;
if(!Graph_StrafeAdmits(candidate->edge2,r->measure.landCell[0],&exit))
    r->outcome="chain_entry";
```

Nothing is widened and no safety check is removed: a chain whose measured
first exit does not admit the second edge is simply no longer priced as that
chain, and the one-hop candidate stands, landing where the next search can
validate the next edge from the state the bot actually has. `chain_entry` is
its own candidate outcome so the refusal is counted and reported rather than
folded into an existing one.

### 7.2 `bot_moveLandReplan`, off by default

Targets the largest single bucket. With the skip instrumentation, the only
gate that ever declines a landing-frame straight search is `off_path_index`
(red 3, blue 5 of their chain breaks): the bot has landed at 370 to 560 u/s,
is not on its path, and `MOVE_PATH_RETRY` (500 ms) must expire before a new
path exists. The long breaks in both cases are 550 to 875 ms, consistent
with that timer, and friction takes the speed inside four frames. The change
treats such a landing as the discontinuity it is and clears the retry, as
`StateAvoid` and the stale-edge branch already do.

### 7.3 What the measurements say

**The run-to-run spread is larger than either effect.** Three matched
repetitions per arm, one build per change, no diagnostic flags, serial:

| case | change | control (ms) | treatment (ms) | mean difference | within-arm spread |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| red outbound | `chainAdmit` | 19150, 20700, 19150 | 19300, 20075, 20075 | +150 | 1550 / 775 |
| blue outbound | `chainAdmit` | 20650, 20650, 20650 | 20750, 19825, 20750 | -208 | 0 / 925 |
| red outbound | `landReplan` | 19150, 19150, 20700 | 19150, 20700, 20700 | +517 | 1550 / 1550 |
| blue outbound | `landReplan` | 18600, 19925, 20650 | 21275, 21275, 21275 | +1550 | 2050 / 0 |

- **`bot_moveChainAdmit` is not resolvable at this sample size.** Its mean
  differences (+150 and -208 ms) are far inside a within-arm spread that
  reaches 1,550 ms. It is neither demonstrated as an improvement nor as a
  regression. An earlier single run per arm read as a 1,025 ms regression;
  that reading was noise and is withdrawn.
- **`bot_moveLandReplan` is a regression on blue outbound.** Its three
  repetitions collapse onto 21,275 ms with no spread at all, against a
  control that reached 18,600 ms. Replanning at once at an off-path landing
  produces a new path from a state the bot cannot exploit and resets the
  parity horizon; it removes the timer without restoring the chain.

Both are retained, both default off, and neither is proposed for the
default. All twelve worlds of each campaign passed with no deaths, no
forbidden spend and zero parity mismatches.

Times here do not vary continuously: they fall into a few discrete outcomes
(20,650 ms three times; 21,275 ms three times; 19,150 and 20,700 ms), so a
run lands in one plan sequence or another rather than jittering around a
mean. Comparisons of single runs between arms are therefore worthless, and
these small samples do not establish a universal detectable-effect threshold.
Report the individual outcomes and matched differences in further trials.

## 9. Conclusions, kept apart

**Replay investigation: open.** The instrumentation and campaigns exist,
but the timing labels, censored comparisons and cross-case conclusions
required correction. Follow the correction record for analyzer-v2 results
and the active handoff for paired successor experiments. The available root
text is not a complete replayable native state, and no single root cause
has been demonstrated.

**Implementation: not qualified.** Two evidence-led corrections were
implemented and measured. `bot_moveChainAdmit` is unresolvable at three
repetitions per arm; `bot_moveLandReplan` regresses blue outbound. Neither
is proposed for the default and both stay off. The working tree's default
behaviour passes the 36-case floor and the 12-case seeded level-1 arm with
no deaths, no forbidden spend and zero parity mismatches, but still fails
the no-legacy gate on one case and is still slower than the greedy arm.

**Sub-15-second target: not achieved.** Best complete runs are 19,050 ms
red and 18,600 ms blue.

### Remaining composition questions

Some planned hops are entered differently from their learned records and
land outside the next edge's entry reach. The executor holds two hops at a
time, but this fact alone cannot attribute the performance gap. The
chain-admission treatment did not show a clear improvement in the retained
small sample; the landing-replan treatment regressed blue. These outcomes
do not establish the untested alternative of preserving distinct records.

### Next executable step

Use [HANDOFF.md](../HANDOFF.md): inspect all retained B-only rescues, then
independently judge censored B candidates from their original roots without
changing A's selection or reference. Test a validated successor only after
that attribution, with matched native routes and all outcomes retained.

A merge experiment needs **prospective** data. `Graph_AddStrafeEdge` discards
losers or overwrites a representative; old graph seeds cannot recover the
folded-away exit states. Capture every entrant, winner, loser and later cost
update with immutable identities, full same-root context and policy records.
Then compare merge/no-merge on that one corpus with controlled budgets and
native continuations. Exit spread alone is correlation, not proof that
merging caused the route error. Preserve state-key merging and entry-state
mismatch as competing explanations.

Do not widen `GRAPH_STATE_REACH`, `GRAPH_STATE_BAND` or `MOVE_STATE_TAKEOFF`,
weaken safety checks, or proceed to level 2/held jumps for this experiment.

### Open questions this record does not settle

- Whether the corridor rule (`NearPathCell`) costs or saves time. In 13 of
  64 sibling pairs the tap-at-once is refused while the run-up is admitted,
  and measured against the objective in force those refused fast hops close
  **+356 u** more on red and **-2,318 u less** on blue, where the worst lands
  off the graph at 20.5 u/s. One-hop progress is not route time.
- The 18 `item_medpak_instant` forecast refusals per red outbound run.
  `bot_forecastItems` exists for them and was deliberately not enabled.
- Why a run lands in one discrete outcome rather than another. The times are
  quantised, not jittered, and nothing here explains the branch.
